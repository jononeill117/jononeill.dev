**Problem.** Jobs were dying in the gap between the vendor portal, the inbox, and the board. Intake was a person. Estimating was a person. The calendar was setting the price.

**What I owned.** Day to day operations: dispatch, job intake, vendor relationships, field teams, and the office team I hired and trained. The P&L all of it lands on.

**What shipped.** Intake rebuilt as an automated pipeline across the vendor work-order portal, the CSM, and VOIP. Scheduling and booking automated so the office is not the bottleneck. SOPs for inventory and estimating, so a new hire can quote without a month of shadowing. Marketing spend pointed at our own booking and revenue data. Commercial work the company was not set up to quote: sewer laterals, multi-crew renovation jobs.

**Who I worked with.** The office team, which I hired and trained. Field technicians. National vendors on the intake side. Gary on CSM review and Steve in the office when a job needed a human in the loop.

**Result.** Average booking time down three hours. Speed to lead up 300%. Weekly revenue up 10 to 15%. Office labor down about 30%, close to $40k a year. $711k annual run rate, January through July 2026 annualized.

This is the seat that changed how I write a roadmap. When you own the number at the bottom, you stop shipping work that only looks good in a review.
